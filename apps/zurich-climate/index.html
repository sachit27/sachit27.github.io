Methodology
Multi-Criteria Spatial Feasibility Assessment (MCSFA)
Why Not S-Curves?

Classical Bass diffusion models assume homogeneous populations and aggregate markets. Here, every building has different physical constraints — a 1884 gas-heated residential building has fundamentally different retrofit potential than a 2016 Fernwärme-connected commercial one. We need a bottom-up, building-resolved approach.

Intervention Priority
Priority = ∛(HeatHazard × PopDensity × CoolingDeficit), factors floored at 0.05

Spatial priority composite via geometric mean: all three factors must be present for high priority. To prevent extreme lows from zero-bounding the index, factors are floored at 0.05. A hot empty warehouse or a cool dense neighbourhood both score low. Heat Hazard uses UHI air temperature from the Theeuwes (2017) equation with SVF and vegetation fraction, not Landsat LST. Socio-economic vulnerability (SoVI) is assessed separately using BFS GEOSTAT demographics.

Adoption Readiness
Proxy: GWR building class → ownership structure (BFS statistics)

Single-family homes (~90% owner-occupied in CH) score high — one decision-maker, no split incentive. Multi-family rental buildings (~85% Mietwohnung) score low due to the tenant-landlord dilemma. Note: Public buildings technically face split-incentives (budget cycles) but in Zürich often bypass standard ROI logic due to Net-Zero 2040 mandates. This highlights the gap between technical suitability and real-world adoption conditions.

True Green Cooling Score
GreenScore = 0.4·TreeVol_norm + 0.6·NDVI_norm

Solely relying on municipal tree inventories creates a massive "public tree bias". We fix this by combining 10m Sentinel-2 satellite NDVI (which captures all vegetation) with Bauminventar tree data, weighted by tree height to proxy canopy volume instead of raw counts. To address MAUP, satellite rasters use windowed median sampling (e.g. 50x50m) rather than raw points. Our PoC validates this with a strong negative NDVI–UHI air temp correlation of r = -0.531 across Zürich.

RSI Formula
RSI = w₁·HeatingScore + w₂·AgeScore
+ w₃·ScaleScore + w₄·CostScore

Weights are scenario parameters. RSI is computed from building heating system, construction period, size, and estimated retrofit cost.

Cost Scenarios (BFE Gebäudeprogramm)
Mindeststandard: ×0.5 | Mittelstandard: ×1.0 | Minergie: ×2.5

All costs are order-of-magnitude estimates from BFE Gebäudeprogramm benchmarks by building class. Three scenarios reflect renovation depth: Mindeststandard (partial envelope + basic heating), Mittelstandard (full envelope + full heating), Minergie (full envelope + heat pump + PV). Actual costs depend on building geometry, soil conditions, grid capacity, and contractor availability.

Data Integration Pipeline

Eight authoritative data layers are fused at building level (via EGID join) into two composite indices. Each building gets both a technical suitability score and a spatial priority score.

🔥 Vulnerability Hotspots
Score = ∛(Heat × Pop × Vuln) × (1 − Green)

Top 50 buildings ranked by combined climate vulnerability: high heat hazard (UHI air temp), high population density (exposure), high socio-economic vulnerability (SoVI), and low green access (cooling deficit). These locations need coordinated intervention: green infrastructure, targeted retrofit programs, and social support for heat protection. Each hotspot shows key stats on click.

Known Limitations
⚠ RSI weights are scenario parameters, not empirically calibrated — use sliders to explore sensitivity ⚠ Cost estimates use BFE Gebäudeprogramm renovation depth scenarios (Mindeststandard/Mittelstandard/Minergie), not fabricated multipliers ⚠ UHI air temp uses Theeuwes (2017) SVF-based equation — not Landsat LST. Air temp ≈ LST−UHImax. Ground-station validated (MeteoSwiss) ⚠ SoVI vulnerability uses BFS GEOSTAT demographics (6 indicators, PCA-based). For full IPCC risk: Risk = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability ⚠ Adoption readiness is a structural proxy from building class, not individual owner preferences ⚠ Canopy radius of 100m (standard in urban ecology, e.g. Ziter et al. 2019)
